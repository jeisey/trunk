## Summary

- **16 activities**, all starting at 3m or 4m. 14 end by 8m and two by 7m. No range goes past 8m.
- **3 busy-nearby options** (watches / same-room / no hands-on / shareable, no water, heat, food or traffic): play-gym batting, toe-catching, and chore chat from the mat. Another 10 are shareable, so a parallel recipe can put them beside a toddler activity.

## Safety and readiness considerations

- **Awake play vs sleep.** Every floor, side-lying, propped, carrier or stroller record says that a drowsy baby goes to their back on their own firm, flat sleep surface (`clm.core.sleep-on-back`). Towel rolls appear only as awake play aids. The side-lying record leaves out AAP's optional folded washcloth under the head, to keep soft items away from the face.
- **Tummy-time records** are always at least arms-reach and never same-room (`clm.core.tummy-awake-supervised`). The three same-room back-play records tell the parent to come close if the baby rolls onto their tummy.
- **Busy-nearby honesty.** "Same-room" for a 3–6-month-old on their back on a floor mat is an editorial classification. No source states a supervision distance. The AAP Back to Sleep page does describe "watching them play on the floor… on their back". Each of these records keeps the mat in the parent's line of sight, away from sofa edges, pets and older children's small toys. Chore chat rules out cooking and hot drinks. The lead should confirm this tier is acceptable.
- **Hanging toys.** AAP's rule is for crib toys (remove when the baby pushes up on hands and knees, or at 5 months). I applied it to floor play gyms as well, so `act.play-gym-bat-and-grab` ends at **5m**, needs short, firm links, and lists the `cords` hazard.
- **Mouthing is expected.** Every toy-based record says to use clean items that are too big to fit in the mouth, with no loose parts, strings or detachable pieces (`clm.b.toys-bigger-than-mouth`). Materials avoid everything on the banned list, and the validator's banned-word scan passes.
- **Handling.** No instruction involves bouncing, tossing, swinging or shaking the baby. The roll-practice, action-song and dressing records each say not to pull on arms or the head. Supported sitting happens on the floor, with the adult's hands low on the trunk and no pillows (see the caveat on `clm.b.supported-sitting`). No jumpers, activity centres or sitting devices are suggested (`clm.b.limit-seats-devices`).
- **Raised surfaces.** The dressing routine and supported sitting cite `clm.b.raised-surface-falls`, and toe-catching and roll practice repeat "floor only".
- **Outdoors.** The walk record uses US (AAP) guidance for under-6-month babies: full shade or canopy, covering clothes and a hat, outside 10 a.m.–4 p.m. on sunny days, and skip it in heat. It also covers carrier face and chin checks, the WHO 1-hour restraint limit, and moving a sleeping baby to a flat surface. Traffic makes it non-shareable.
- **Readiness.** Abilities are used only where they matter: `head-control` for roll practice and supported sitting. Readiness notes point families of babies born early or with medical needs to their clinician and to corrected age. The towel-roll record says plainly that it does not count toward a flat-surface target.
- **Milestones** are only described ("many babies", "timing varies"). No record uses a milestone as a deadline or a target.

## Conflicts and jurisdiction differences

Implications in B:
- `act.tummy-time-towel-roll` is `propped`, `flatSurface: false`. Its readiness note says it does not count toward a flat-surface target. ### Other differences

- **Sun under 6 months.** AAP's general Sun Safety page (2024) says to keep babies under 6 months out of *direct* sunlight in shade. It allows sunscreen on small areas if no shade is available. AAP's Baby Sunburn Prevention page (2026, Section on Dermatology) is stricter: babies under 6 months should be kept out of *direct and indirect* sunlight *entirely* because of heat-stroke risk. Read literally, that would rule out any daytime outing. HSE (IE) advises shade for all babies under **12** months, with peak UV 11 a.m.–3 p.m. CPS (CA) does not recommend sunscreen under 6 months and gives 10 a.m.–2 p.m. as peak. The walk record follows the conservative US reading: fully shaded, outside 10–4, skip in heat. **Unresolved:** the lead should decide whether the 2026 AAP wording means a 4–6-month walk should be dropped altogether.
- **Hanging toys.** AAP's 5-month removal rule is for crib toys. HSE recommends a mobile above a baby at play (not over the cot). I applied the AAP cut-off to floor gyms as a conservative choice.
- **Device limits.** NHS gives a 20-minute limit for walkers, bouncers and seats. WHO limits any restraint, strollers and carriers included, to 1 hour. SickKids (NICU follow-up) says to avoid standing devices. AAP pages say to limit swings and bouncy seats without giving a number.
- **Peekaboo timing.** CDC suggests peekaboo at 4 months. AAP lists interest in peekaboo as a 9–12-month skill. The record frames it as a game a young baby may just watch and smile at.
- **Books.** AAP's How Active page suggests fabric or board books at 3–6 months. AAP's Share Books page names board and fabric books for 6–8 months and "bold pictures" for birth to 6 months. These are compatible, and the record uses both.
- **Supported sitting.** AAP mentions supporting the back with pillows. Trunk uses the adult's hands and lap instead, to avoid soft items near a baby who may tip forward. This is noted in the claim caveat.
- **Terminology.** US "diaper" is used in Trunk text; Irish and UK sources say "nappy".

## Ideas omitted or softened, and why

- **Lap bouncing or supported standing.** CDC 4 months suggests holding a baby under the arms with feet on your lap and letting them bounce, and CDC 6 months suggests supported standing. Both were omitted: Trunk does not give bouncing instructions, and SickKids advises avoiding standing devices.
- **AAP "Simple Ways" ideas for 4–6 months.** The knee ride ending with a drop ("Mustang Sally") was omitted as a fall and handling risk. The chest rocking idea is already covered by core `act.tummy-time-chest`. The homemade blocks (cartons stuffed with paper and taped) were omitted because mouthing tape or wet cardboard is an ingestion and choking risk. A "bat the tower" activity with soft blocks was considered and dropped, since it overlaps play-gym batting and the rattle record.
- **Backyard shade-blanket time** was omitted. The stricter 2026 AAP sun wording applies, and ground-level hazards (insects, sticks and small objects a mouthing baby can reach) need closer supervision. Workstream C has a 6m+ grass-blanket record.
- **Kitchen narration** was changed to chore chat away from cooking, under the burns policy: no heat, no hot drinks, and babywearing is not a cooking position.
- **Passing a toy hand to hand** was omitted. AAP places this at 6–8 months, which is outside the band.
- **Baby massage** was omitted. Workstream A covers hand-and-foot massage, and the page makes broad health benefit claims Trunk shouldn't repeat.
- **ZERO TO THREE "lift baby's feet toward the mouth to encourage rolling"** was not used, because the WebFetch rendering made its framing unclear. Roll practice instead uses CDC and HSE "toys just out of reach" with a gentle hip guide, and is labelled `adapted`.
- **Glass mirrors and cloth-over-face peekaboo** were excluded. The mirror must be an unbreakable baby mirror, and in peekaboo the adult hides their own face.
- **Age ranges trimmed to avoid same-idea overlap with Workstream A (1w–4m).** Babble chat, the action song, mirror, side-lying, walk and toe-catching now start at **4m**. My original diaper-change chat collided with A's `act.diaper-change-chat` and duplicated its idea, so I replaced it with a distinct dressing routine (`act.dressing-sleeve-peekaboo`). The lap book now ends at 6m so it doesn't overlap C's `act.point-and-name-books` (6m+).

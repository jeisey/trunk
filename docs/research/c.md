# Workstream C research notes: babies 6 to under 12 months

Owner: Workstream C. Content file: `content/workstreams/c.json`. Sources checked 2026-10-28.

## Scope and method

- **What's here:** 16 activities whose main age range is 6 to under 12 months. Five run past 12 months, but only where Workstream D has no equivalent record that starts at 12m. Ranges were trimmed so each record hands off to B (which ends at 8m) and D (which starts at 12m) without near-duplicate suggestions.
- **How pages were read:** each page was opened and read on 2026-10-28.
  - HealthyChildren.org, HSE, NHS and Just One Norfolk pages were downloaded and converted to text.
  - The CPSC playground handbook PDF was extracted with `pdftotext` and read in full for the sections cited.
  - cdc.gov and cpsc.gov web pages refuse command-line downloads (Akamai returns 403), so they were read with the WebFetch tool. That tool returns a model-rendered text of the page, and the passages used were re-queried for exact wording. **A browser spot-check of the CDC and CPSC passages is recommended** (see "For the lead").
- **No food activity:** this file has no snack or feeding activity. Food is left to later workstreams (see "Ideas omitted").
- **No clinical review:** these records are based on sources, not reviewed by a clinician. Durations, setup and cleanup times, and effort levels are editorial estimates. The AAP observation informed them: about 2–3 minutes per toy at 8 months, up to about 15 minutes by 12 months (AAP Cognitive 8–12 months). That observation is not entered as a claim.

## Safety and readiness considerations

- **Choking (the main risk at this age).**
  - Every object-play record says items must be too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube (HSE home check), and the US small-parts rule is cited alongside it.
  - No record lists `small-parts`, and the validator forbids it for under-3s anyway.
  - The treasure-basket and safe-room records name the usual offenders (keys, coins, batteries, small balls, small lids) in safety notes only, never in materials or steps. They also tell parents to check under furniture and between cushions and to keep older children's small toys out.
  - Mouthing is described as expected, not discouraged.
- **Suffocation.**
  - Peekaboo puts the cloth over the parent's head or the toy, never the baby's face (CDC: nothing that could cover the face).
  - The cushion course is not shareable and requires arm's reach, because AAP warns a baby can fall between pillows or under a box and smother.
  - Play-yard notes carry the AAP set-up rules: sides up and mesh intact, hanging toys removed once the baby sits or gets on all fours, climbing aids removed once the baby pulls up.
- **Falls.**
  - Floor play only. Dressing and changing happen on a floor mat, with "never leave on a changing table, bed or sofa" (AAP, NHS, HSE).
  - Cruising requires stable or anchored furniture with no sharp corners, gated stairs, climbable furniture kept away from windows, and no wheeled walkers.
  - Swings must be full bucket seats over impact-absorbing surfacing, with the parent lifting the baby in and out.
- **Furniture tip-over.** The cruising and safe-room records require tip-prone furniture and TVs to be anchored (AAP, HSE).
- **Burns.**
  - The pots band is "never while you're cooking."
  - The play yard is presented as the place for the baby while a parent cooks, instead of holding or wearing them (HSE: never cook while holding your baby; CDC, AAP, NHS: never carry hot drinks or food while holding a baby).
  - Babywearing is treated as holding, so it is not a cooking position (see caveat on `clm.c.hot-drinks-cooking`).
- **Water.** `act.splash-and-pour` is set up as follows:
  - leads, arm's reach, continuous hands, not shareable, and no `parent-moment` intent;
  - safety notes for touch supervision, taking the baby along if you must leave, bath seats not being safety devices, testing the temperature, and emptying water straight away;
  - lists `water` and `heat` (scald) hazards.
- **Outdoors.**
  - Sun guidance for babies over 6 months (shade, covering clothes, hat, sunscreen allowed) on the blanket, walk and swing records.
  - Lawn-treatment and poisonous-plant notes on the blanket record.
  - Stroller harness, brake and never-unattended rules on the walk; carrier face-visible rules for babywearing walks.
  - Traffic is listed on the walk, which is therefore not shareable.
- **Gentle handling.** No bounce, toss, swing-the-baby, jiggle or shake wording anywhere. The songs record carries CDC's "never shake" safety claim. The swing uses "small, slow pushes" of the swing seat.
- **Readiness.**
  - Abilities are used instead of age alone: `sits-independently` for container, pots, treasure basket, ball, water and swing; `crawls` for the cushion course and safe room; `pulls-to-stand` for cruising.
  - The crawling claim notes that scooting and slithering count, and the cushion-course readiness note says so.
  - No milestone ages are stated as targets. AAP's "not sitting by nine months" and NHS's "not moving by 12 months" prompts were deliberately not repeated.
  - Questions about individual development go to the clinician (scope of `clm.c.crawling-varies`). Records do not repeat a prematurity note on every card; the core tummy-time records already carry one.

## Conflicts and jurisdiction differences

1. **Peekaboo cloth placement.** AAP's first peekaboo variation drapes a soft cloth over the baby's head, but CDC's infant safety list says not to let babies play with anything that could cover their face. CDC's 9-month list puts the cloth over the parent's head. **Trunk follows CDC:** the cloth goes over the parent or a toy, never the baby.
2. **Bed as a changing surface.** AAP's 8–12-month movement page suggests retiring the changing table in favour of "the floor or bed". NHS and HSE say never leave a baby on a bed. **Trunk uses the floor only.**
3. **Treasure-basket contents.** Just One Norfolk's example list includes keys and a bouncy ball. AAP lists small balls as a choking hazard, and NHS and AAP warn about button batteries, which car-key fobs can contain. **Trunk excludes keys and small balls.** The basket is still described by Just One Norfolk, so the activity stays "described" with this caveat.
4. **Bouncing.** HSE's how-to-play page lists "bounce them on your knee" for 3–6 months. Trunk's policy forbids bouncing instructions; nothing from that bullet is used.
5. **Play-yard time limits.** AAP's 2009 playpen page calls a playpen a safe place when parents can't watch every moment. HSE recommends a playpen only for short periods and never unsupervised. CPSC gives no time guidance. **Trunk takes the stricter line:** the parent stays in the same room, checks often, and the duration is a short estimate (5–20 minutes).
6. **Sun.**
   - AAP (US) allows sunscreen on all exposed skin after 6 months and keeps under-6-month-olds out of direct sun.
   - Records start at 6 months, so the over-6-month rule applies.
   - The sunburn advice (call the doctor if a baby under 1 is sunburned) is kept as a caveat.
   - Other countries' sunscreen guidance was not researched.
7. **Bath water temperature.**
   - AAP recommends a hot-water heater maximum of 120°F; NHS gives no number but says to run cold water first.
   - Trunk states only the shared advice: test with your wrist or elbow, and empty the water afterwards.
8. **Terminology.** CPSC's playground handbook calls 6–23-month-olds "toddlers". The handbook dates from 2010 (with a 2015 notice) and is written for playground operators.
9. **Stroller sleep.** AAP's stroller page says that if a child falls asleep in a stroller, keep them in sight. AAP safe-sleep guidance (core `clm.core.sleep-on-back`) says to move a baby who falls asleep in a seat, swing or carrier to a firm, flat surface as soon as possible. **Trunk gives both:** keep them in view while out, then move them to a firm, flat sleep space.
10. **Poison help numbers differ by country.** The AAP backyard page gives the US Poison Control number. `clm.c.yard-hazards` says "contact poison help" without a number.

## Ideas omitted or softened, and why

- **Food and snack activities: omitted.** They are left to later workstreams, as allowed. CDC's 9-month and 1-year lists include finger foods and cups, but feeding needs `eats-solids` readiness, allergy notes and seating, and Trunk gives no feeding protocols.
- **Hide-the-toy as a separate record: folded into peekaboo** as an adaptation. Workstream D's `act.hide-the-toy-hunt` starts at 12m, and a separate 8–12m record would be a near-duplicate.
- **Sock basket and laundry helper: omitted.** D has `act.sock-basket-helper` (15m+), and laundry brings detergent-pod poisoning and drawstring hazards that would need their own sourced notes.
- **Kitchen narration while babywearing: rejected.** HSE says never cook while holding your baby. The kitchen case is covered by `act.play-yard-in-view` instead.
- **Mirror play: omitted.** B covers it with `act.mirror-faces-together` (3–8m). A 6–12m version would be a renamed duplicate.
- **Bubbles (AAP "Playing outside"): omitted.** Bubble solution and wands raise ingestion questions at a mouthing age, and D covers bubbles from 12m.

- **"Swinging calms children" (AAP): softened.** It is kept as a general description with a caveat that it is not infant-specific evidence. It is used only for the bucket swing's `sensory` goal.
- **Playground beyond the bucket swing: omitted.** Slides, steps and similar equipment start in D (`act.playground-toddler-area`, 12m+, `walks`).
